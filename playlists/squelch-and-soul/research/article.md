# Squelch & Soul — The Acid Thread Through the Garage Lineage

**41 records, five decades, one sound worn five different ways.** The Roland TB-303 is
remembered as the voice of acid house — but the squelch never belonged to acid house alone.
This playlist follows the acid bassline *around* the genre named after it: through soul,
disco and boogie; through Chicago and New Jersey's vocal house; through UK garage, speed
garage and 2-step, where the squelch got jungle pressure behind it; through Sheffield
bassline, filter house and space disco; and into the 2020s garage revival, where a new
generation reaches for the same rubber. Dancefloor cuts only, at least five per decade,
played in chronological order so the thread tells itself.

## What counts as "acid" here

The brief is the **sound, not the genre**: a TB-303, a clone, or an analogous squelchy
analogue bassline — resonant, rubbery, alive under a filter — doing dancefloor work inside
the soulful lineage: **soul, disco, garage, the Jersey sound, soulful & deep house, garage
house, UK garage, speed garage, 2-step** and, by agreement, four near neighbours: **bassline
/ Niche 4x4, UK funky, nu-disco & disco edits, and filter/French house**. Acid house proper
is admitted only when its heart is soulful (a Jamie Principle or Paris Brightledge vocal
buys a lot of forgiveness). Named **Acid Mixes and Acid Dubs count** — the acid often lived
on the B-side. Where a bassline warps rather than strictly squelches (speed garage's
signature move), the note says so honestly.

The **1980s pool** uses full adjacency as agreed: soulful Chicago/NY club records over
squelchy analogue bass, proto-acid Italo/boogie, and vocal acid crossovers — because before
1987 the squelch simply hadn't reached the soul cluster yet.

## The five movements

**I. Before the letter (1983–1989).** Alexander Robotnick's rubbery MC-202 line shows the
squelch already living inside Italo disco. Chicago then gives it a soul: Adonis plays the
303 like a bass guitar, Jamie Principle moans over analogue snakes, Ralphi Rosario puts a
diva on top, and by 1988 the crossover is explicit — Maurice *sings* the craze, DJ Pierre
(Phortune) makes acid lush, Julian Jonah quietly invents the London version, and Lil Louis
takes the squelchiest bassline in this document into the pop charts.

**II. The squelch goes to the club (1991–1998).** Strictly Rhythm is the bridge: DJ
Pierre's Wild Pitch (Photon Inc.) and Armand Van Helden's Witch Doktor put filtered squelch
under New York garage. Cajmere's Underground Goodie mix does the same for gospel-house in
Chicago; Daft Punk funk it up; and then the UK mutation — Armand's Sneaker Pimps and Tori
Amos remixes, Ripgroove, Serious Danger, Industry Standard — turns acid's rubber into speed
garage's warp: the same analogue elasticity with jungle pressure behind it.

**III. Garage's acid decade (2000–2008).** The thread runs literal (Visionary's *Acid
Flex* — a 303 over a UKG skip; DJ Narrows' *Saved Soul*, the warp bassline every
revivalist still studies) and then north: T2 and DJ Q carry the Niche/bassline strain,
while the disco end squelches on through Chicken Lips, LCD Soundsystem's 303 meltdown,
Lindstrøm's space disco and Kerri Chandler's resonant stabs.

**IV. Revival one (2012–2018).** The garage-house resurgence puts squelch back on UK
floors — Julio Bashmore, Shadow Child, Breach — while Paranoid London prove acid house
proper can still carry a Chicago soul vocal, and the nu-disco wing (Todd Terje, Krystal
Klear) turns the arpeggiated squelch into melody.

**V. Revival two (2021–2024).** The UKG/speed garage revival and its neighbours reach for
the same circuits: Ewan McVicar's Chaka Khan flip, Kolter's bumping garage-acid, DJ
Seinfeld and Confidence Man's acid-pop, Lauren Flax's literal *Acid Jacker*, and Joy
Orbison's *Flight FM* — the wriggling rubber bassline as 2024's biggest club sound.

- **The 90s NY/NJ "Acid Dub" seam** — the era's soulful house 12"s hide named acid dubs on
  B-sides (Mood II Swing, MAW's orbit). No specific dub earned a row this pass; a
  crate-level sweep of Strictly Rhythm / Nervous / Emotive B-sides would deepen Movement II.
- **UK funky (2008–2013)** — sanctioned as a neighbour, but no specific track proved a
  genuine acid line this pass; the seam stays open.
- **Swami Sound – Hope It Stays (Kaisui Remix) (2024)** — press notes "LFO acid" in this
  NYC garage-revival cut; digital-only and thinly catalogued, so it waits for an anchor.
- **X-Press 2 – Say What! (1993)** — kept in the table with an 🎧 flag: the acid undertow
  deserves an ear-confirmation against the London Underground Mix.
